How to read this

  • Parking is a rule anchored on this season's eight observed fills (two weekends by eyewitness), not a fitted model. Weekend + decent conditions centres on ~08:35; weekday powder days ~09:30; fresh snow, pent-up demand after washed-out weekends and school holidays pull it earlier; poor visibility or a wind hold pushes it later and cuts the odds. The chance is shrunk toward the season base rate the further out we look.
  • Visibility is where GFS puts the cloud relative to the 1,400–2,100 m skiing band, cross-checked against NIWA's ridge-height visibility at Mt Cheeseman (2,031 m, 35 km away - a proxy, not Mt Hutt) and MetService's text where it reaches.
  • Wind is ridge-level (GFS 800 hPa, ~1,950 m), which is what puts the upper lifts on hold. HIGH has gone with weekend crowds collapsing to ~74% of expectation; a nor'wester lowers the threshold. The LOW / MODERATE / HIGH label is a band, but the parking maths underneath rises smoothly with wind, so a few km/h either side of a boundary no longer flips the answer. When the models disagree sharply about the wind, the fill chance is pulled toward the season average and the time window widens.
  • Crowd model: gradient boosting on 2022–24 traffic counts, leave-one-season-out R² 0.53. It under-calls the biggest days.
  • NZ daylight saving starts Sun 27 Sep - all times are local clock times either way.

The models, and how far to trust each

  • GFS — the US global model (NOAA), ~25 km grid, four runs a day landing roughly 4:30am, 10:30am, 4:30pm and 10:30pm NZ, out to 16 days. The only feed that gives us cloud by height and wind at ridge level, so the visibility call and the hold-risk band both rest on it. Its habit here is to under-call wind when the others see a nor'wester, and its cloud placement is shaky in nor'west arch conditions - the webcams have caught it both ways.
  • ECMWF — the European model, in the open 25 km version, two full runs a day landing about 8am and 8pm NZ, out to 15 days. It moves the weekend picture more than anything else, which is why the two issues sit just after it arrives. Usually the most skilful global model and the one to side with when it splits from GFS on wind. What we get has no cloud-by-height and no freezing level.
  • ICON — the German model (DWD), ~13 km, four runs a day at about the same times as GFS (~4:30am, 10:30am, 4:30pm, 10:30pm NZ), but only out to about 7½ days, so it drops out of the second weekend. Tends to run strong on wind; a useful tie-breaker inside a week.
  • NIWA — New Zealand's own model chain (1.5 km to two days, coarser after), read at Mt Cheeseman - 2,031 m and 35 km up the range - because there is no public point on Mt Hutt. Refreshes about twice a day (the run we pull in the evening was issued from midday). Hourly to three days, 3-hourly after; visibility and gusts stop at about six days. The best instrument for ridge-height visibility and gusts inside three days, but it is a proxy on a different mountain.
  • MetService — a meteorologist's sentence for the ski field, republished by NZSki, two days ahead only. It shows up when NZSki edit their page, usually 6–8am, with afternoon tweaks. Over 47 days it's flagged 17 of 20 bad-visibility days the night before, with 8 false alarms - nearly all of them snow turning up late in the day. Treated as the human check, not a model.
  • Webcams — not a forecast input - a camera can't see Saturday - but the marking scheme. The base and summit cameras are sampled at 9am, midday and 3pm each night and scored for visibility by how much edge detail survives in the frame (a whiteout has none; snow fools brightness measures, so those aren't used). Every visibility call above - the GFS layer call, MetService's text, NIWA's visibility - is checked against those frames after the fact, and the forecasts are archived as issued so the check is fair: GFS rewrites its own history between runs. MetService's clean record so far comes from that scoring; the GFS layer call doesn't have enough archived days for a number yet.
  • Weighing them — inside three days: NIWA and MetService for visibility and wind, ECMWF for the big picture, GFS for cloud height. Four to seven days: ECMWF and ICON, with GFS noted as the dissenter. Beyond a week only GFS and ECMWF reach, everything is a lean, and the second weekend will move. When they split on wind, GFS has been the optimist this season - Sat 19 Sep is the live test.

This is an independent forecast, not an official one. It is put together from public weather models and NZSki's own live feed, and nobody at the ski field checks it. For the road, chains and whether the mountain is open, always check mthutt.co.nz on the day.
